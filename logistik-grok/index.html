What we do

Four parts of the same route.

  1. 01

    Freight

    Road and ocean freight are booked as one movement. The truck is already assigned when the container is named, so the quay is a handoff, not a waiting room.

  2. 02

    Warehousing

    Inbound goods are checked, given a location, and released against an order. A pallet can be found without walking the aisle to ask who put it there.

  3. 03

    Last-mile delivery

    Routes are built the evening before. The driver leaves the dock with the load and the paperwork already matched, and exceptions are reported before the customer calls.

  4. 04

    Supply chain

    Planning ties the warehouse, the sailing, and the delivery into one schedule. When a vessel slips, the truck plan moves with it instead of being discovered at the gate.

Teams

The people who keep the handoffs.

  • Operations

    Owns the daily plan. They confirm sailings, assign trucks, and protect the handoff times the rest of the company has already promised.

  • Warehouse

    Receives, stores, and picks. They know which aisle a pallet is in and which order it has to leave on.

  • Drivers

    Run the road legs and the last mile. They leave with a loaded truck and a route, and they report a delay before the customer discovers it.

  • Planning

    Builds the week. They match capacity to bookings and rewrite the plan when a ship or a road is late.

Purpose

One team from the warehouse door to the last delivery.

Northline exists so a shipment has one responsible team from the warehouse door to the ship and on to the final stop. The road leg, the quay, and the last mile are planned as a single route.

Mission

We commit to named handoffs: freight leaves with a confirmed vessel, stored goods have a location and an order, and last-mile routes depart on the day we published.

Customers see the same plan we run on the floor — what is booked, where it sits, and when the truck rolls.
